Artificial intelligence inference is the process of running a trained machine learning model to generate predictions or outputs from new input data. Unlike training, which requires massive computational resources to update model weights over large datasets, inference focuses on efficiently executing a fixed model to produce results in real time or near-real time. As AI models have grown from millions to hundreds of billions of parameters, the challenge of performing fast, cost-effective inference has become one of the central engineering problems in the field. Large language models such as GPT, LLaMA, Qwen, and DeepSeek are autoregressive transformer architectures. During inference, they process an input sequence through a series of attention layers and feed-forward networks to produce a probability distribution over the vocabulary at each step. The model then samples or greedily selects the next token, appends it to the context, and repeats the process until a stopping criterion is met. This sequential decode phase is fundamentally memory-bandwidth-bound because, at each step, the model must read all of its parameters from memory to compute just a single output token. To accelerate inference, hardware vendors have developed specialized accelerators. NVIDIA's A100 and H100 GPUs deliver high memory bandwidth and support for tensor cores that accelerate matrix multiplications in mixed precision.
